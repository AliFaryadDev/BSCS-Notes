## Introduction

Behavioral aspects of decision making explain how human emotions, experiences, habits, and mental limitations influence managerial decisions.

Managers do not always make decisions through perfect logical analysis.

## Definition

Behavioral decision making refers to the influence of human behavior and psychological factors on the decision-making process.

## 1. Bounded Rationality

Bounded rationality means that managers have limited information, time, and mental ability when making decisions.

**Example:** A manager cannot examine every available supplier because of limited time.

## 2. Satisficing

Satisficing means selecting the first acceptable solution instead of searching for the absolute best solution.

**Example:** A company selects a supplier that meets its basic requirements at a reasonable price.

## 3. Intuition

Intuition is the ability to make decisions based on experience, feelings, and instinct rather than detailed analysis.

**Example:** An experienced manager quickly recognizes a potential business problem.

## 4. Escalation of Commitment

Escalation of commitment occurs when managers continue supporting a failed decision because they have already invested time, money, or effort in it.

**Example:** A company continues funding an unsuccessful project because it has already spent a large amount of money.

## Common Decision-Making Biases

### 1. Overconfidence Bias

Believing that one's knowledge or ability is greater than it actually is.

### 2. Confirmation Bias

Paying more attention to information that supports existing beliefs.

### 3. Anchoring Bias

Depending too heavily on the first piece of information received.

### 4. Availability Bias

Making decisions based on information that is easily remembered.

### 5. Hindsight Bias

Believing after an event that its outcome was easy to predict.

## Conclusion

Behavioral factors influence how managers make decisions. Understanding these factors helps managers avoid common mistakes and improve decision quality.
