## Introduction

A strategy is a plan of action designed to achieve organizational goals and gain a competitive advantage.

Organizations develop strategies at different levels to coordinate their activities.

## Definition of Strategy

Strategy is a long-term plan that explains how an organization will use its resources to achieve its objectives.

## Levels of Strategies

There are three major levels of organizational strategy.

### 1. Corporate-Level Strategy

Corporate-level strategy is developed by top management and determines the overall direction of the entire organization.

**Main Decisions:**
- Which businesses should the organization operate?
- Which markets should it enter?
- Should it expand or reduce its operations?

**Example:** A company decides to enter the telecommunications industry.

### 2. Business-Level Strategy

Business-level strategy focuses on how a particular business competes in its market.

**Main Decisions:**
- How can the business attract customers?
- How can it compete with rivals?
- How can it create competitive advantage?

**Example:** A mobile company offers lower prices to attract customers.

### 3. Functional-Level Strategy

Functional-level strategy focuses on how individual departments support business-level strategies.

**Main Departments:**
- Marketing
- Finance
- Human Resources
- Operations
- Information Technology

**Example:** The marketing department develops an advertising campaign to increase sales.

## Difference Between Strategy Levels

| Corporate-Level | Business-Level | Functional-Level |
|---|---|---|
| Entire organization | Individual business | Individual department |
| Overall direction | Market competition | Departmental activities |
| Top management | Business managers | Functional managers |

## Conclusion

Corporate, business, and functional strategies work together to achieve organizational objectives and maintain competitive advantage.
