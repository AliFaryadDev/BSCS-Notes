## Introduction

Establishing reporting relationships is an important part of organizing. It defines who reports to whom and explains the authority relationships within an organization.

## Definition

Reporting relationships define the formal lines of authority and communication between managers and employees in an organization.

## Organizational Hierarchy

Organizational hierarchy is the arrangement of positions according to levels of authority and responsibility.

**Example:**

1. CEO
2. Department Manager
3. Supervisor
4. Employees

Each level has specific responsibilities and reporting relationships.

## Important Concepts

### 1. Chain of Command

Chain of command is the formal line of authority that connects employees with higher levels of management.

**Example:**

Employee → Supervisor → Manager → CEO

### 2. Unity of Command

Unity of command means that each employee should report to one direct supervisor.

**Purpose:**
- Avoids confusion.
- Prevents conflicting instructions.
- Clarifies responsibility.

### 3. Span of Control

Span of control refers to the number of employees directly supervised by one manager.

**Example:**

If one manager supervises 10 employees, the manager's span of control is 10.

## Types of Span of Control

### A. Narrow Span of Control

A manager supervises a small number of employees.

**Characteristics:**
- More management levels.
- Close supervision.
- Greater administrative cost may occur.

### B. Wide Span of Control

A manager supervises a large number of employees.

**Characteristics:**
- Fewer management levels.
- More employee independence.
- Requires effective communication and capable employees.

## Tall and Flat Organizational Structures

### 1. Tall Structure

A tall structure has many management levels and usually a narrow span of control.

**Advantages:**
- Close supervision.
- Clear authority levels.

**Disadvantages:**
- Slower communication.
- More management costs.

### 2. Flat Structure

A flat structure has fewer management levels and usually a wider span of control.

**Advantages:**
- Faster communication.
- Fewer management levels.
- More employee independence.

**Disadvantages:**
- Managers may become overloaded.
- Less individual supervision.

## Conclusion

Establishing reporting relationships creates clear authority, communication, and responsibility. It helps employees understand whom they report to and improves organizational coordination.
