## Introduction

Group and team decision making involves two or more people working together to identify problems, discuss alternatives, and select solutions.

It allows organizations to use the knowledge and experience of different individuals.

## Definition

Group decision making is a process in which multiple individuals participate in making a common decision.

## Advantages of Group Decision Making

1. More Information: Members contribute different knowledge and information.
2. More Alternatives: Different people suggest different solutions.
3. Better Understanding: Members understand problems from different perspectives.
4. Acceptance: Employees may support decisions they helped create.
5. Creativity: Group discussions can produce new ideas.

## Disadvantages of Group Decision Making

1. Time-Consuming: Discussions may take a long time.
2. Conflict: Members may disagree with each other.
3. Groupthink: Members may avoid disagreement to maintain harmony.
4. Dominance: One person may control the discussion.
5. Pressure: Members may feel pressure to agree with the majority.

## Techniques of Group Decision Making

### 1. Brainstorming

A technique in which members freely suggest ideas without immediate criticism.

**Purpose:** Generate many possible solutions.

### 2. Nominal Group Technique (NGT)

A structured technique in which members first generate ideas individually and then discuss and rank them.

**Purpose:** Ensure participation from all members.

### 3. Delphi Technique

A technique in which experts provide opinions through multiple rounds of questionnaires, usually without meeting face-to-face.

**Purpose:** Develop a collective expert opinion.

### 4. Electronic Meetings

Meetings in which members use technology to share ideas and make decisions.

**Purpose:** Support communication and decision making through digital tools.

## Team Decision Making

Team decision making involves members of a team working together toward a shared objective.

**Characteristics:**
- Shared responsibility.
- Open communication.
- Cooperation.
- Common goals.
- Mutual support.

## Difference Between Group and Team

| Group | Team |
|---|---|
| Members may have individual goals. | Members work toward common goals. |
| Individual responsibility is common. | Shared responsibility is important. |
| Coordination may be limited. | Members coordinate closely. |
| Results may be individual. | Results are usually collective. |

## Conclusion

Group and team decision making improve participation, creativity, and problem solving, but managers must control conflicts and avoid groupthink.
