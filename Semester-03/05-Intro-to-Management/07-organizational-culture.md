## Introduction

Organizational culture refers to the shared values, beliefs, attitudes, and practices that guide the behavior of people within an organization.

## Definition

Organizational culture is a system of shared values and beliefs that influences how employees behave and perform their work.

## Elements of Organizational Culture

### 1. Values

Values are the principles that an organization considers important.

**Example:** Honesty, teamwork, and customer satisfaction.

### 2. Beliefs

Beliefs are ideas that employees consider true about the organization.

### 3. Norms

Norms are informal rules that guide employee behavior.

### 4. Symbols

Symbols are objects, signs, or images that represent an organization's identity.

### 5. Stories

Stories are events and experiences shared among employees that communicate organizational values.

### 6. Rituals

Rituals are regular activities and traditions followed by an organization.

## Characteristics of Organizational Culture

1. Shared: Culture is shared by organizational members.
2. Learned: Employees learn culture through experience.
3. Transmitted: Culture passes from one employee to another.
4. Behavioral: It influences employee behavior.
5. Unique: Every organization may have a different culture.

## Types of Organizational Culture

### 1. Strong Culture

A strong culture exists when employees widely share and follow organizational values.

### 2. Weak Culture

A weak culture exists when organizational values are not strongly shared among employees.

## Importance of Organizational Culture

- Creates organizational identity.
- Guides employee behavior.
- Improves teamwork.
- Increases employee commitment.
- Supports organizational goals.
- Creates a positive working environment.

## Conclusion

Organizational culture plays an important role in shaping employee behavior and creating a common identity within an organization.
