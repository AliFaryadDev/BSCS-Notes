## Introduction

Rational decision making is an approach in which managers carefully analyze available information and alternatives before selecting the best possible solution.

## Definition

Rational decision making is a systematic process of selecting the best alternative based on logical analysis and objective evaluation.

## Rational Decision-Making Model

### 1. Identify the Problem

Clearly recognize and define the problem.

### 2. Establish Decision Criteria

Identify the factors that are important for making the decision.

### 3. Assign Weights to Criteria

Give importance or priority to each decision criterion.

### 4. Develop Alternatives

Identify possible solutions to the problem.

### 5. Analyze Alternatives

Evaluate each alternative according to the established criteria.

### 6. Select the Best Alternative

Choose the alternative that provides the best overall result.

### 7. Implement the Decision

Put the selected alternative into action.

### 8. Evaluate the Decision

Review the results to determine whether the decision was successful.

## Assumptions of Rational Decision Making

1. Clear Problem: The problem is clearly defined.
2. Complete Information: All relevant information is available.
3. Known Alternatives: All possible alternatives can be identified.
4. Clear Preferences: Decision makers understand their priorities.
5. Logical Thinking: Decisions are based on objective reasoning.
6. Best Outcome: The decision maker selects the best possible alternative.

## Limitations of Rational Decision Making

- Complete information is rarely available.
- Time and resources may be limited.
- Human thinking has limitations.
- Future conditions are uncertain.
- Some problems are too complex.

## Conclusion

Rational decision making provides a logical and systematic approach to selecting solutions, but real-world limitations may prevent managers from making perfectly rational decisions.
